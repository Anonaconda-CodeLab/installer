Install Android apps from your computer

Plug your phone in with a USB cable, choose an app file, and install it. It's free, there's no account, and the file never leaves your computer.

This browser can't talk to phones over USB. Open this page in Chrome, Edge, or another Chromium browser on a computer or Android phone. Firefox and Safari don't support it, and neither do iPhones or iPads.

  1. Get your phone ready

    You only need to do this once per phone.

    1. On the phone, open Settings → About phone and tap Build number seven times. You'll see “You are now a developer”.
    2. Go to Settings → System → Developer options and turn on USB debugging.
    3. Unlock the phone and plug it into this computer with a USB cable that carries data.

    The menu names vary a little between phones. On some, Developer options is directly in Settings.

  2. Connect

    Your browser will list the phones it can see. Pick yours and choose Connect.

    Look at your phone. Tap Allow on “Allow USB debugging?”. Tick “Always allow from this computer” to skip this next time.

    No prompt? Unlock the phone, unplug it, plug it back in, and connect again.

    Connected to your phone.

  3. Choose the app

    Download the app from its install page, then choose the file here. We check it's exactly the build we published before installing.

      None of our apps has a public release yet, so there's nothing to check against.

      We can't vouch for apps that aren't ours. An app can read what you give it and use any permission you allow. Only install files from publishers you trust, downloaded from their own site.

    If something doesn't work

    My phone isn't in the list when I click Connect
    • Unlock the phone and check that USB debugging is on.
    • Try another cable. Many cables, especially ones that came with chargers or power banks, only carry power and can't connect to anything.
    • Pull down the phone's notification shade. If it says “Charging this device via USB”, tap it and try File transfer.
    • Try another USB port, ideally one directly on the computer rather than a hub.
    Windows doesn't see my phone

    Windows needs a USB driver before a browser can talk to a phone in debugging mode. For Pixel phones and many others, install Google's USB driver. Samsung, Xiaomi and some other makers have their own; Google keeps a list of manufacturers' drivers. Unplug and replug the phone afterwards.

    Linux says access denied

    Your system needs Android udev rules so your user account can open the phone. Fedora 42 and newer include them. On other distributions they're often packaged as android-udev-rules or android-sdk-platform-tools-common. Install them, then unplug and replug the phone.

    If you use Chrome or Chromium as a Flatpak, it may not be allowed to see USB devices. Give it device access (for example with Flatseal), or use the version from your distribution's packages.

    It says another program is using the phone

    Only one program at a time can talk to the phone. Close Android Studio, scrcpy, and any other phone tools. If you've used adb in a terminal, its background server keeps hold of the phone: run adb kill-server, then unplug and replug the phone.

    Which browsers work?

    Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera, Vivaldi and other Chromium-based browsers, on Windows, macOS, Linux, ChromeOS and Android. Firefox and Safari don't support WebUSB, which this page needs, and iPhones and iPads can't do it at all.

    Can I install from one phone to another?

    Often, yes. Open this page in Chrome on the first phone and connect it to the second phone with a USB-C to USB-C cable. The second phone needs USB debugging turned on, as in step 1. If the phones just charge each other, unplug the cable and plug it in the other way round, or change the USB setting from the notification shade on either phone. Not every phone supports this.

    What happens to my file and my data?

    Nothing is uploaded. This page is a set of plain files: it has no tracking, no cookies, no analytics, and no server of its own. The app file goes straight from your computer to your phone over the cable, and its checksum is worked out on your computer.

    To connect, your browser creates a key and stores it only in this browser, so your phone can remember it when you tick “Always allow”. You can delete it here, and remove the approval on the phone in Developer options with Revoke USB debugging authorizations.

    What's a SHA-256 checksum?

    It's a 64-character fingerprint of a file. If even one byte of the file changes, the fingerprint changes completely. When a publisher lists the checksum of their app, you can compare it with the one shown here to make sure your file is exactly theirs.

    Should I turn Developer options off afterwards?

    Yes. When you've finished installing, go to Settings → System → Developer options on the phone and switch it off at the top. That also turns off USB debugging, so no computer can use the connection. Your installed apps keep working. Turn it back on the next time you install or update an app this way.

    Why this exists

    Google is rolling out a rule that apps on certified Android phones must come from developers who have registered and verified their identity with Google. It began in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand in September 2026 and is due to reach the rest of the world in 2027. Installing over a USB cable with ADB, as this page does, still works for apps from developers who haven't registered.
